Platforms are the architecture's tiers, top to bottom: presentation, application, domain, data, integration. Each file is sorted into one by what its code uses (UI, HTTP or a command line, nothing but logic, storage, another program's API); where the code shows nothing, its path decides; [tiers] in the config overrides both. A platform's size comes in steps and grows with the files on it. Its rooms stand on the plots of a street grid, the same on every platform, near the rooms they work with.

Rooms are openings in a platform. By dependencies: files of one tier that depend on each other and change together, whatever folder they are in. By folders: the folders the code is filed in, a big folder split into its sub-folders; the plots of a component's rooms are tinted. A component sits near the same spot on every platform and in both views. Switch between them and watch the files move: a folder that is one thing moves as a block, a folder that is several things scatters.

Folders vs rooms (the panel): how far the two agree, folders split across several rooms, rooms that gather several folders, and files whose dependencies live in another folder. Colour the strata by Folder to see it on the map: one colour per component, a shade per sub-folder.

Strata: below each opening hangs a pit, as deep as the room's code. Every band on the pit's walls is one file, as thick as its share of the room's lines, the biggest on top; it darkens with depth. Its colour is chosen above (language, recent changes, duplication, complexity).

Form: as buildings, each room stands on its platform instead, as high as its pit would be deep; a floor is a file, the biggest at the bottom, and every dependency is a pipe, in the streets of a platform, each in its own lane, and down onto the roofs between platforms. The marks below are on the roofs.

Corridors on a platform, tubes between platforms carry the dependencies between rooms, wider for more; red where two rooms depend on each other, dashed lavender where they only change together.

On a room's floor: pins are magic values, hatching duplicated code, dark discs swallowed errors, an orange cone a layer violation; a dashed red rim a dependency cycle; warm glass a hotspot. Orange arcs tie rooms that hold clones of each other.

Cross-section (right): cut the stack at a tier. The tiers above it are cut away, that tier is solid and labelled, the ones below are ghosted. Alt + scroll moves the cut.

Hover a room for its numbers, click it for its files and findings, double-click it for its class diagram. The metrics table and the coupling matrix are below the map.
